Noise Versus Signal

By Kate Ziehm, President, Morning Ag Clips

PUBLISHED ON July 18, 2025

GREENWICH, N.Y. — Recently, I started listening to The Diary of a CEO, a podcast hosted by Steven Bartlett.

The title ‘CEO’ sounds so big, legitimate, and highfalutin.  It feels so un-agricultural.

So, why does this pertain to you, farmer, ag lender, crop consultant, Farm Bureau president, etc.? In agriculture, most of us touch small ag business (or large) and are in or have occupied leadership roles.  

It doesn’t matter if you are directing the chopping crew or the milking parlor team, the junior lending officers, or fellow crop consultants; someone has to take the lead.  If it’s you leading and navigating the ship, this is something you should consider—noise versus signal.

Today, upon listening, I was inspired to think about focus.  

Guest, Kevin O’Leary, from Shark Tank, discussed a signal-to-noise ratio, a concept introduced to him through his work with Steve Jobs.   

As Jobs explained it to Kevin, signal (for Steve Jobs) was the top 3-5 things that he had to accomplish in the following 18 waking hours that he had.  That’s all.   No further out than that. 

Jobs deemed it critical to accomplish those 3-5 tasks within that period to support the mission.  Anything other than that was considered noise and wasn’t moving him towards his overall goals.  

For Steve Jobs, operating at an 80/20 signal-to-noise ratio was optimal.

Jobs recognized the finite number of hours that we are given to work with every day.  Understanding that time could not be wasted, he set the signals every day and continued until they were completed.  It was the only way he could ensure that things continued to move forward, as he saw it. 

I see this concept as something we all struggle with in today’s fast-paced, multitasking, information-driven world—which is at our fingertips 24-7!

It’s overwhelming.  And, as I reflect on the productivity of my days, I realize how many of them I’ve spent getting sidetracked by one thing or another—adding up to a whole lot of noise.  Currently, I would consider my signal-to-noise ratio to be the complete opposite of Steve Jobs, probably 20:80.  Ooh, I have some work to do. 

It’s a constant battle.  

Noise in my inbox.  Noise on my phone through notifications, texts, apps, and calls.  Kids, friends, family, the dog, house chores…and the list goes on.

Highly successful people, leaders, and entrepreneurs possess the ability to cut through all the noise and get straight to the signal, which is the objective of what they want to accomplish on a given day, every day.  The signal keeps it all moving forward.

Unwavering and constant.  I challenge you to identify your daily signals and focus on them.  Don’t do it just because you read this small reminder today, but do it every day.  See how much further you can get toward meeting your longer-range goals.

It takes 45 days to form a habit. I am going to list my top 3 signals every night before I go to bed and set out to reach them every day following, in an attempt to get better at drowning out the noise.

Dial in and try it.  Stay committed to it.  

Signal vs. Noise.  80:20.  Go on now, do it!

Have a great day, everyone!  

—Kate Ziehm, President, Morning Ag Clips
