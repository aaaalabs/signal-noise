The Hidden Productivity Principle That Powers Visionaries Like Jobs and Musk

What if productivity isn’t about time at all?

Martin Messier
4 min read
·
Jul 4, 2025

28 years ago, Steve Jobs reclaimed the helm of Apple and began a ruthless transformation.

This week, Kevin O’Leary shared what he learned from working with Jobs on The Diary of a CEO podcast.

Today, I’m sharing the single most important productivity lesson I picked up from that conversation.

Not a member yet? You can read this story for free here.

It had nothing to do with time management. Or goal setting. Or work-life balance.

It’s about knowing the difference between signal and noise and making sure the noise doesn’t win.

This idea shaped how Jobs ran Apple. It shaped how O’Leary leads his companies.

And if you take it seriously, it will change how you work too.

The signal to noise ratio: what it means and why it matters

Steve Jobs is famous for obsessing over product design. But it wasn’t his only fixation. He also obsessed over focus. And the concept that helped him guard that focus was what he called the signal to noise ratio.
